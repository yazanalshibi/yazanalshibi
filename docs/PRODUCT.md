# Product Overview

## 1. Business concept

Build an AI-native Market Intelligence Platform that creates a living, machine-readable representation of a market.

The platform combines public, commercial, proprietary, geographic, economic, demographic, behavioral, product, pricing, competitor, and consumer data into one continuously evolving **Market Intelligence Data Foundation**.

That foundation supports multiple intelligence capabilities:

`UNDERSTAND → DISCOVER → SIMULATE → TEST → OPTIMIZE → VALIDATE → LEARN`

The product is **not** only a synthetic-user tool and **not** only a market-research database.

**Long-term objective:** become a **Market Twin** — an environment where businesses can understand what exists in a market, what consumers need, how they currently behave, what alternatives they use, what they are willing to pay, and how they may respond when an offer changes.

**Core principle:** First understand the market from evidence. Then simulate possibilities. Then validate against reality.

## 2. Problem

Companies make decisions with fragmented information. A business considering a new product may separately research demographics, market size, competitors, reviews, pricing, surveys, search trends, locations, economic conditions, personas, and industry reports — then manually combine them with assumptions.

Existing synthetic-consumer platforms solve only part of this. Simulation becomes far more valuable when agents are grounded in a continuously maintained representation of the actual market.

**Opportunity:** infrastructure connecting

`Market Evidence + Consumer Evidence + Synthetic Populations + AI Reasoning + Simulation + Real-World Calibration`

## 3. Operating modes

| Mode | Prompt shape | Outcome |
|------|--------------|---------|
| **IDEA → MARKET** | “I have this concept. Investigate it.” | Evidence-backed evaluation + counterfactuals |
| **MARKET → IDEA** | “Show me problems and opportunities.” | Need/gap discovery from evidence |

## 4. Primary user groups (later)

Entrepreneurs, existing businesses, franchisors, retailers, consultants, investors, product teams, marketing teams, economic development organizations.

**Initial users:** entrepreneurs and small/mid businesses testing offers in GTA automotive services (narrow vertical for validation, not as product identity).

## 5. North-star experience

> “I want to launch a $149 express detailing service in Scarborough.”

The platform responds with evidence about who needs it, why, current alternatives, likes/dislikes, rejection reasons, price resistance, feature importance, preference durability, switching drivers, geographic differences, supporting evidence, and confidence — then supports counterfactuals (“What if it’s $129?”, “What if pickup is included?”, “What if we move to Mississauga?”).

Later, reverse mode: “Don’t test my idea. Show me what this market needs that existing businesses aren’t adequately providing.”

## 6. Example vertical (seed domain)

**Automotive detailing in Scarborough** is the development environment because it provides many businesses, prices, reviews, geographic variation, repeat purchases, and competitive alternatives — plus real-world validation opportunities.

It does **not** make the platform an automotive product. Architecture remains industry-agnostic.

### Example input

- Premium automotive detailing package
- Scarborough
- $189
- Interior + exterior
- 2.5 hours

### Desired output structure (not “67% like it”)

1. **Observed market context** — competing offers, coverage, review themes, demographics, historical pricing  
2. **Simulated reaction** — estimated acceptance under defined assumptions (labeled SIMULATED)  
3. **Needs** — convenience, appearance, resale protection, time savings, cleanliness  
4. **Primary barriers** — price, service duration  
5. **Alternatives** — DIY, automatic wash, cheaper detailer, delay  
6. **What would need to change?** — experiment hypotheses  

### Counterfactual experiment workspace

| Version | Change |
|---------|--------|
| Baseline | $189, customer brings vehicle |
| A | $159 |
| B | $189 + pickup/drop-off |
| C | $169 + 90-minute service |
| D | $189 + quarterly membership |
| E | $149 basic + optional upgrades |

Same synthetic population evaluates all versions to isolate what changed the response.

## 7. Differentiation

| Platform type | Gives you |
|---------------|-----------|
| Research database | Data |
| Synthetic-user tool | Simulated people |
| Analytics | What happened |

**Market Twin connects:**

- What exists?
- What happened?
- What do people need?
- Why are they behaving this way?
- What might happen if something changes?
- What could satisfy the need better?
- What happened when we tested that assumption in reality?

The last question closes the loop and builds the moat.

## 8. Core assets (moat)

1. **Market Knowledge Graph** — businesses, products, consumers, needs, prices, geography  
2. **Historical Market Database** — how markets changed over time  
3. **Need Graph** — problems, desired outcomes, barriers, alternatives  
4. **Synthetic Population Infrastructure** — representative artificial populations  
5. **Experiment Database** — scenarios tested and simulated responses  
6. **Calibration Database** — simulated expectations vs actual outcomes (**hardest to reproduce**)

## 9. Platform flywheel

```
More Data
  → Better Market Representation
  → Better Experiments
  → More Real Tests
  → More Actual Outcomes
  → Better Calibration
  → Better Market Representation
```

| Enterprise | Private datasets and custom market models |

## 11. Proprietary data option

Businesses may connect CRM, POS, website analytics, feedback, transactions, advertising, inventory, and operational data to enrich **their** market model without automatically becoming shared/public data.

Separation required:

- Global / Public Market Intelligence  
- Private Company Intelligence  

with access controls end-to-end.
